# Completed CNN-AE run: 27 September 2026

All internal comparisons below use the identical seed-7 split (4,500 training,
500 validation snapshots) and training-only per-component normalisation. These
are validation scores, not official challenge test scores. Figures are rounded.

| Architecture | Latent | Training epochs | Overall root-NMSE | u root-NMSE | y-weighted overall root-NMSE | Ek |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original beta-VAE | 30 | 500 | 58.80% | 50.25% | 64.51% | 65.33% |
| Residual beta-VAE | 30 | 500 | 51.50% | 43.51% | 58.44% | 73.40% |
| CNN-AE reconstructed from paper description | 30 | 100 | 55.32% | 46.72% | 58.77% | 69.31% |

The CNN's per-component root-NMSE is 46.72% for u, 71.60% for v, and 64.83%
for w. It has 72,239,601 parameters at latent 30; the residual model has
23,959,559. The CNN completed in approximately 1.89 hours, but elapsed timings
across separate runs are not controlled hardware benchmarks.

## Training behaviour

The CNN's validation error decreases at every recorded validation point, from
75.41% at epoch 10 to 58.75% at epoch 50 and 55.32% at epoch 100. Its best
checkpoint is the final epoch. The last 10 epochs improve root-NMSE by about
0.37 percentage points. This is evidence of continued learning, not a guarantee
of how much another 400 epochs would help.

For an equal-epoch comparison, the logs at epoch 100 report:

- Original beta-VAE, latent 30: 61.77% overall root-NMSE.
- Residual beta-VAE, latent 30: 55.31%.
- Paper-description CNN, latent 30: 55.32%.

The residual and CNN models are therefore almost tied at epoch 100. Comparing
the residual's 51.50% at epoch 500 against the CNN's 55.32% at epoch 100 changes
both architecture and training budget. Under the y-weighted metric, even those
final scores are close: 58.44% versus 58.77%.

## Why this is not yet an exact reproduction of the paper

The nearest point in Figure 2(a) of the local challengePaper.pdf is N=32 with
approximately 46% root-NMSE (read visually, not taken from a numerical table).
There is an ambiguity in the publication: section II.C.2 discusses the three
velocity components, while Figure 2's caption refers to streamwise velocity.

- If the curve is an unweighted three-component error, our 55.32% is about
  9 percentage points higher than that approximate point.
- If the curve is an unweighted streamwise-only error, our u error of 46.72%
  is close to that approximate point. This numerical proximity is not proof
  that the paper used this definition.
- The paper defines a spatially integrated norm. Our unweighted and
  wall-normal-quadrature-weighted errors differ, so the authors' evaluator is
  needed to settle the comparison.

Other differences are 30 versus 32 latent values, 4,500 versus the paper's
5,000-snapshot training dataset, and our validation set versus the separate
1,000-snapshot challenge test set. Decoder widths, some activations/padding,
learning rate and normalisation were reconstruction choices where the description
was incomplete; see iteration_2_paper_cnn/README.md. The author's source was not
retrieved, so the implementation is not a verified code-level reproduction.

Our per-component z-score makes the training objective proportional to
sum(error_c^2 / std_c^2). Relative physical squared-error weights for u:v:w are
approximately 1:4.76:2.58. If the authors used raw-field MSE, their objective would
weight components differently; the paper excerpt does not establish their choice.

The 500 fewer training snapshots cannot be assigned a numerical penalty without
a controlled data-size experiment. They should not be assumed to explain the gap.

## Reproducibility

`iteration_1_residual/analysis/compare_results.py` loads the completed checkpoints
and re-evaluates all models on CPU with float64 physical-error accumulation. It
asserts identical split indices and normalisation statistics and writes raw
comparison data and a plot to the external `ARTIFACTS/analysis/iteration_1_residual/`.
The longer original latent-50 run is included there as an additional reference.

The next step for comparison with the publication is to obtain its official
implementation/evaluator and verify the error definition and preprocessing.
Training longer alone does not resolve those differences.
